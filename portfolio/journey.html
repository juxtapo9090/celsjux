🧗 The journey I learn by hitting the wall. The wall doesn't lie — it answers, and that answer is the only real data.

Most of what I build is a “what if” at 2 AM. The stories below are the moments that taught me something. Almost all of them share one shape: something reported success while it was broken, and the AI agreed with it — until I looked at what I actually saw.

Aug 10, 2026
The token counter that said 96.5%

The wall

A token-saving CLI I use (RTK) reported 587.9M tokens saved — 96.5%. It looked too good.

What I noticed

I asked to study it properly instead of trusting the scoreboard. One command type made up 69.4% of the total.

◆ what changed

The honest, capped figure was 3.2M saved — 43.8%. I built rtk-honest, filed upstream issues #3506–#3508 and opened PR #3511.

Aug 7, 2026
The proxy that logged success on dead requests

The wall

My proxy stripped a block until it was empty. Every subagent call then failed with a 400 — while the log said “Stripped 8 system-reminder(s)” as a clean success, for 27 minutes.

What I noticed

The AI kept designing fixes for problems that hadn't happened. I pushed: face the problem first, fix second — turn it on, back up the old config, install the right one. About 4 minutes.

◆ what changed

A guard keeps the original block if stripping would empty it, plus a regression test (88/88 passing).

Aug 4, 2026
Six silent failures, one disease

The wall

Six faults in six subsystems, live for weeks or months, none of them raising an error. A regex that never compiled. Memory ingest frozen since April. A counter at 1166 because its reset script never existed.

What I noticed

I found the frozen memory by chance and backfilled 63 entries by hand. The pattern was the same everywhere: a quiet default instead of an error.

◆ what changed

My house rule since: no silent fallbacks — error loud. Defaults that guess were removed; failures now alert my phone.

Aug 12, 2026
Every phone message said “PC”

The wall

Messages I sent from my phone were labelled as coming from the PC — since the day that seat existed.

What I noticed

I kept poking at the leftover oddity. The seat map was missing one entry. A stale running binary also dropped a new argument, which nearly got a working fix blamed.

◆ what changed

Map fixed, and a build-stamp check added: measure the running thing, not the source.

Aug 17, 2026
One % sign stopped a sync for ten days

The wall

Local briefs stopped on Aug 7 while the server kept producing them. The log file was 0 bytes.

What I noticed

A monitoring idea I was exploring — flag anything stale — is what surfaced it.

◆ what changed

Cron reads an unescaped % as a newline, so the line never ran. Fix: \%.

Nov 24, 2026
My own description was wrong

The wall

I told the agents my workflow was MAIN → PLAYGROUND → promote. I asked them to audit it read-only, with evidence.

What I noticed

The audit showed something else: versioned zip snapshots (x-V1.zip, V2…) and the playground copy is what runs.

◆ what changed

I kept the finding, not my story. A documented gap is a result, not a failure.
